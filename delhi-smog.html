Delhi's Winter Smog Crisis

Chemical Processes, Health Impacts, and Emission Controls

Every winter, Delhi transforms into one of the world's most polluted cities. As temperatures drop and winds calm, a toxic blanket of smog envelops the metropolitan region, affecting over 30 million residents.

Critical Warning: The Air Quality Index (AQI) regularly exceeds 400—classified as 'severe' or 'hazardous'—with PM 2.5 concentrations reaching 15-20 times the World Health Organization's safe limits.

This annual crisis is not merely an environmental problem but a severe public health emergency that costs thousands of lives and affects the development of millions of children.

The Science of Smog Formation

Understanding Winter Smog

Delhi's winter smog is a complex mixture of particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), carbon monoxide (CO), ozone (O₃), and volatile organic compounds (VOCs).

The Role of Temperature Inversion

The critical factor is temperature inversion. Normally, air temperature decreases with altitude. However, during winter nights, a layer of cold, dense air is trapped beneath warmer air, creating an atmospheric lid that prevents vertical mixing and traps pollutants in the breathing zone.

Chemical Processes

Sulfur Dioxide Transformation

Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) from coal combustion undergoes transformation into sulfate particles:

Aqueous Phase Oxidation:
SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃
H₂SO₃ + ½O₂ → H₂SO₄

In daylight, hydroxyl radicals (OH•) facilitate gas-phase oxidation, but winter's weak sunlight severely limits this cleansing mechanism.

Nitrogen Oxides Chemistry

NOₓ plays a dual role. During the day, NO₂ photolysis creates ozone (O₃), but at night, fresh NO emissions from traffic deplete ozone.

Nitric Acid Formation:
NO₂ + OH• → HNO₃
HNO₃ + NH₃ → NH₄NO₃ (Ammonium Nitrate)

Devastating Health Impacts

Systemic Failure: PM2.5 penetrates deep into the lungs and enters the bloodstream, triggering systemic inflammation and oxidative stress.

Cardiovascular & Respiratory Damage

For every 10 μg/m³ increase in PM2.5, heart attack risk increases by 1-3%. Chronic exposure leads to irreversible lung damage and accelerated lung function decline, particularly in children.

Neurological Risks

Ultrafine particles can cross the blood-brain barrier, triggering neuroinflammation. This is linked to accelerated cognitive decline in adults and neurodevelopmental risks in children, affecting academic performance and behavior.

Emission Control Measures

Because atmospheric chemistry cannot cleanse the air in winter, controls must happen at the source:

  • Transportation: BS-VI standards and Electric Vehicles.
  • Industry: Electrostatic precipitators and Flue Gas Desulfurization.
  • Agriculture: Happy Seeders to eliminate crop residue burning.

Conclusion

Delhi's winter smog is a humanitarian crisis. The solution requires aggressive, sustained emission reductions across all sectors—transport, industry, power, and agriculture—and strong regional coordination to ensure the fundamental right to breathe clean air.
